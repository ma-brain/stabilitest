# R Journal Submission Revision Implementation Plan

> **For Claude:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task.

**Goal:** Replace the historical Welch interpretation bands with a prospectively
frozen, method-specific train/held-out calibration decision, revise the R
Journal article to match that decision, and produce a technically clean,
reproducible submission archive for a CRAN-published version of `stabilitest`.

**Architecture:** Add a self-contained `welch_unpaired` calibration study by
adapting the proven study harness under `manuscript/calibration/studies/`. The
study freezes its protocol, fits and hashes one candidate using training data,
then evaluates held-out data once. A single published verdict drives the runtime
registry, article artifacts, manuscript wording, and final CRAN-version check;
failure produces numeric-only Welch output rather than revised gates.

**Tech Stack:** R >= 4.2, `testthat`, `stabilitest`, `rjtools`, R Markdown,
Pandoc, LaTeX/RJournal.sty, Git, Poppler (`pdfinfo`, `pdftoppm`), and the
existing calibration-study conventions in this repository.

  `docs/plans/2026-08-10-r-journal-welch-recalibration-design.md`.

  archive is assembled. Do not add CRAN submission work to this plan.
- Treat the historical Welch 55/70 bands and the 12-scenario R Journal
  simulation as design inputs and comparators only.
- Never inspect validation scores before the candidate hash is committed and
  the human approves the validation opening.
- Never change a frozen gate, truth definition, scenario, weight, or candidate
  after validation is opened.
- A failed calibration changes Welch to `uncalibrated`; it does not trigger a
  fourth attempt inside this plan.
- Do not upload to CRAN, the R Journal, win-builder, or any other external
  service. The final archive and reports are handed to the human.
- All manuscript numbers must be computed in R chunks or loaded from committed
  artifacts generated by committed scripts.

**Step 3: Write and freeze the SAP**

The SAP must define, before any score pilot or production run:

- the exact calibration unit: significant `welch_unpaired`, mean difference,
  `alpha = 0.05`, weights `(0.4, 0.4, 0.2)`, `n_boot = 1000`, removal cap 0.30;
- core normal-data archetypes covering per-group sizes 25, 50, 100, and 200,
  allocation ratios from 2:3 through 3:2, and variance ratios 1:1 and 1:2;
- null, borderline, and clear truth strata, with borderline and clear effects
  determined by frozen 60% and 95% Welch-power targets;
- independent training and validation scenario rows with different seeds;
- stress-only Student-t, skewed, and gross-contamination rows;
- conditional-on-significance sampling because runtime labels apply only to
  significant conclusions;
- 250 completed significant analyses per required truth/scenario cell;
- attempted, screened, completed, failed, and excluded counts;
- candidate hierarchy: three-band, then two-band, then no mapping;
- frozen tie-breaking and the rule that any emitted non-fragile verdict counts
  as reassurance;
- false reassurance, clear identification, balanced ordinal accuracy, ordering,
  occupancy, and failure gates from the approved design;
- one-sided Wilson bounds plus scenario-cluster bootstrap bounds, with the more
  conservative bound governing the decision;
- candidate hashing and a single validation opening;
- `welch-2026-2` as the version only if validation passes;
- the exact fail-closed registry action if validation fails.

Use these six core archetypes in both training and validation, with different
scenario IDs and seed ranges:

| W03 | 100 | 100 | 2 | 1 |
| W04 | 200 | 200 | 1 | 1 |
| W05 | 40 | 60 | 1 | 2 |
